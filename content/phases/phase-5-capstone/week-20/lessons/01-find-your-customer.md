## What you're about to do

You will pick one real person with one real problem. Then you will send them a short message asking for 30 minutes. That person becomes your customer for the next three weeks.

## Why a real customer

Hiring managers see many demo projects. Few of them were used by someone who is not the builder. A real customer gives you messy data, changing needs and honest feedback. That is the job of a Forward Deployed Engineer.

Your customer does not need to be a big company. Good options are close to you. A physiotherapy clinic in Pune that loses patients to no-shows. A CA firm in Surat that types GST invoices into Tally by hand. An NGO in Kochi that tracks volunteers in three Excel sheets. Your college exam office. Your uncle's hardware shop. A friend's support team.

Look for three things. The person feels the pain every week. They can give you access to sample data, legally and with consent. They have one hour a week to talk to you.

## A small example

Here is a short outreach message that works:

```text
Hi Anjali ma'am, I'm Priya, a software learner from Kothrud.
Meena mentioned that missed appointments cost the clinic a lot.
I'm doing a free 3-week project to build a small tool for a real
business. Could I visit for 30 minutes on Thursday to understand
how bookings work today? No cost, and you keep whatever I build.
```

It names a pain, asks for a small amount of time and makes the offer clear.

## Common mistakes

The first mistake is picking a problem you find interesting but nobody feels. If nobody asks "when will it be ready?", the project will stall.

The second mistake is promising too much. Do not say "I will automate your whole clinic." Say "I will try to fix one thing."

## On the job

FDEs often start with a customer who did not ask for them. Getting that first meeting, and keeping it small and useful, is a skill you will use every quarter.
