## What you're about to do

You will practise a discovery call with a simulated clinic manager. Then you will write up notes from your own real customer meeting.

## Ask open questions

Discovery is about how work happens today. You want facts, numbers and stories. You do not want the customer to agree with your idea.

Open questions start with what, how, when, who or tell me. "Walk me through what happens when a patient books." "How many people missed appointments last month?" "What did you try before?"

Closed or leading questions get short answers. "Do you use WhatsApp?" gets "yes". "Isn't a chatbot a good idea?" gets a polite yes that means nothing.

Ask for numbers early. How many, how often, how long, how much in rupees. These numbers become your baseline in week 22. Without a baseline you cannot prove impact.

Ask about data last. Where does it live? Who owns it? Can you see a sample with names removed?

## A small example

```text
You:      Walk me through a normal Monday at the front desk.
Manager:  We open at 9. Meena calls everyone booked for the day.
You:      How many calls is that?
Manager:  Around 40. It takes her most of the morning.
You:      How many patients still don't turn up?
Manager:  Maybe 7 or 8 a day.
```

In three turns you found the workflow, a time cost and a rough no-show rate.

## Common mistakes

Pitching too early is the big one. If you describe your solution in the first ten minutes, the rest of the call is about your idea, not their problem.

Another mistake is not writing things down. Write their exact words. A quote like "Monday mornings are just phone calls" is gold for your case study.

## On the job

At a customer site, the first week is mostly discovery. The FDE who asks better questions builds the right thing first time.
