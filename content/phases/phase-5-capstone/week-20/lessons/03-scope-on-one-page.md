## What you're about to do

You will turn your notes into a problem statement, data rules and a one-page scope. Your customer will read it and sign it.

## Small scope wins

You have about two weeks of build time. That is enough for one useful thing, not five. Your scope says what you will build, what you will not build and how you will know it worked.

A good problem statement names who feels the pain, how often and what it costs. "The front desk spends about 3 hours each morning calling 40 patients, and 18% still miss their slot."

The success metric must be something the customer can say in their own words. "Fewer no-shows" is fine for them. You add the number: from 18% to under 10% in four weeks.

Data rules matter in India. The DPDP Act expects consent and care with personal data. Patient names and phone numbers are personal data. Write down where data is stored, who can see it and when you delete it.

## A small example

```text
MVP: WhatsApp reminder sent 24 hours and 2 hours before each slot,
with a reply button to confirm or cancel.
Out of scope: online payments, doctor notes, a mobile app.
Timeline: build by 14 Nov, pilot 15-28 Nov, review 29 Nov.
```

The out-of-scope list is just as important as the MVP. It protects you when the customer asks for "one more small thing".

## Common mistakes

A scope with no metric cannot be finished. You will keep building forever.

A scope without data rules can hurt the customer. Never copy real patient data to your laptop without permission.

## On the job

A statement of work (SOW) is how FDE teams agree on what a project is. Writing a clear one page is often worth more than a week of code.
