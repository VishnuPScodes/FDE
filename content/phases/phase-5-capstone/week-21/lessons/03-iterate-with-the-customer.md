## What you're about to do

You will run a weekly check-in with a simulated customer who brings a new requirement. Then you will log the change and show real usage.

## Requirements will change

Every real project changes once real people touch it. That is a good sign. It means they are using it. Your job is to understand the change, show its cost and let the customer choose.

In a check-in, ask what they saw, what worked and what did not. When a new request comes, ask why. Ask how many people it affects. Ask what can move to make room for it. Then write it down as a change request with impact on the timeline.

You need at least one handled requirement change in your case study. Hiring managers look for it.

## A small example

```text
Change request CR-1 (18 Nov)
Request: reminders in Marathi as well as English
Why: about a third of patients are over 60 and prefer Marathi
Impact: +1 day; message templates need approval again
Decision: accepted; doctor-wise report moved to next phase
```

## Common mistakes

Saying yes in the meeting and finding the cost later is the classic mistake. The customer hears "free and immediate".

Another is treating a change as a complaint. "The reminders are too early" is useful data, not an attack.

## On the job

Most FDE work is this loop. Ship, watch, listen, adjust, and keep a written record so nobody is surprised.
