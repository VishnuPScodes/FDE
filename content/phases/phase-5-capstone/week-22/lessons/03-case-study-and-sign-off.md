## What you're about to do

You will collect feedback from your customer, write a two-page case study and get a signed-off form that closes the project.

## Tell the real story

A case study is not a feature list. It follows the work. Context, then discovery, then how you broke the problem down. Then the architecture, what broke, the impact in numbers and what you would do next.

"What broke" is the part hiring managers read most closely. It shows judgment. Say what went wrong, how you found it and what you changed.

Get a short quote from your customer. Ask open questions: "What changed for you?" "What would you tell another clinic?" Ask permission before you publish their name.

Record a three-minute video walk-through if you can. Show the system working, not slides.

## A small example

```text
"Meena now spends her mornings with patients, not on the phone.
No-shows have halved." - Dr. Anjali Sharma, Sharma Physiotherapy
```

## Common mistakes

Writing only about the technology is the big one. Start with the customer's problem.

Publishing customer data by mistake is the other. Blur names and phone numbers in screenshots.

## On the job

Every FDE interview asks "tell me about a project". A clear case study with a real customer and real numbers is the answer you will give again and again.
