## What you're about to do

You will write a measurement plan, calculate before and after numbers from real records, and spot a misleading metric.

## Same measure, same window

Impact is a comparison. You need a baseline from before your system and the same number after it. Measure both the same way, over windows of the same length.

Your baseline should come from the customer's own records. For the clinic, that is four weeks of the booking sheet before the pilot. Count booked slots and no-shows. Divide. That gives about 18%.

After the pilot, count again over four weeks. If the rate is now 9%, you can say "no-shows fell from 18% to 9%". That is a drop of 9 percentage points, or half. Say both clearly. People mix these up.

Add one number about people's time, too. "Morning calls went from 3 hours to 40 minutes" is easy for anyone to understand.

## A small example

```text
Before (13 Oct - 9 Nov): 1,040 booked, 187 no-shows -> 18.0%
After  (17 Nov - 14 Dec): 1,010 booked,  91 no-shows ->  9.0%
Change: -9.0 percentage points (-50% relative)
```

## Common mistakes

Comparing different windows is the most common error. Diwali week has fewer bookings. If only your "before" window has Diwali, your result is not fair.

Another mistake is counting activity instead of outcome. "2,000 messages sent" is activity. Fewer no-shows is the outcome.

If the number did not improve, say so. An honest "no change, and here is why" is still a strong case study.

## On the job

Customers renew when they can see impact in their own numbers. A clear before and after is how an FDE earns the next project.
