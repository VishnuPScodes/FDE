## What you're about to do

You'll write short messages for senior people: a recommendation memo, a weekly update, and a bad-news memo. Each one has to be read in two minutes by someone with forty other things to do.

## The idea

Executives read the top, then stop. So put the answer first. This is called bottom line up front.

A good memo has five parts. Recommendation says what you want them to do. Why gives two or three reasons with numbers. Cost and timeline says how much and how long. Risks names the biggest ones honestly. Decision needed says exactly what you need from them and by when.

For a weekly update, use a status colour. Green means on track. Amber means at risk but fixable. Red means we need help. Then say what was done, what is next, what is blocking, and what you need.

Bad news needs the same discipline. Do not hide it in paragraph four. Say what happened, how big the impact is, why it happened, what you are doing about it, and what decision you need. Take ownership. Do not blame the customer's team.

Write short sentences. Use rupees and dates. Avoid fancy business words.

## A small example

> Recommendation: Approve a 4 week pilot of WhatsApp reminders in two clinics, costing ₹60,000.
> Why: No-shows cost about ₹4.5 lakh a month. A 4 point drop saves ₹1 lakh a month.
> Decision needed: Yes or no by Friday 14 March.

## Common mistakes

Starting with background. The CEO knows the background.

Asking for "thoughts". Ask for a decision, and say what happens if they wait.

Colouring everything green. If you hide amber, you will be forced to show red later.

## On the job

Trust grows when bad news arrives early, with a plan attached.
