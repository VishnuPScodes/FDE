## What you're about to do

You'll practise two customer conversations where the customer wants more than you can deliver. Then you'll write a reply that moves the extra requests to a later phase without upsetting anyone.

## The idea

Scope creep usually starts with a friendly sentence. "Can you also add billing?" Saying yes to everything breaks the deadline. Saying no flatly breaks the relationship.

Instead, ask questions. What is the real deadline, and what happens if it is missed? Which part matters most? What can wait a month? Who decides the priority? When the customer says it out loud, they often drop the extras themselves.

Then offer a trade. "We can add billing, but that moves the go-live from 10 March to 14 April. Or we keep 10 March and add billing in phase 2." Two options, each with a clear cost. The customer chooses.

Write the agreement down the same day. A short note with three parts is enough: what is in, what is deferred, and the next step.

Keep tone warm and plain. "Good idea, and here is where it fits" works better than "That is out of scope."

## A small example

> Owner: We need everything by Diwali.
> You: What is the one thing that must be ready by Diwali?
> Owner: The audit on 15 November needs matched invoices. The rest can wait.

One question moved the scope from five features to one.

## Common mistakes

Agreeing in the meeting and worrying later.

Offering only one option. A trade needs two.

Forgetting to write it down. Memory shifts after a week.

## On the job

Scope is the most common cause of unhappy customers. Handle it early and kindly.
