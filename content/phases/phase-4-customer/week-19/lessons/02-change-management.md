## What you're about to do

You'll plan how a team will actually start using a new tool. You'll talk to a worried user, write an adoption plan, and draft a short staff guide.

## The idea

A tool that works but is not used has failed. Adoption is part of your job.

Start with people. Every team has champions, who like new things and will try it early. It also has sceptics, who worry. Both groups matter. Champions show it works. Sceptics find the real problems.

Worry is usually about three things: "Will I lose my job?", "Will I look foolish if I get it wrong?" and "Who do I ask when it breaks?" Ask open questions and listen. Do not argue. Often the person's real worry is smaller than you thought, and easy to fix.

An adoption plan has five parts. Champions: who goes first. Training: short and hands on, thirty minutes with real work. Fears and objections: what people said, and your answer. First 30 days: weekly check-ins and a place to report problems. Success signal: one number that shows it is being used, such as the share of files that go through the tool.

Write a one page staff guide: what changes, what stays the same, how to get help, and what to do when the tool is wrong.

## A small example

Lalitha, a senior clerk at a co-operative bank, fears the tool will take her work. After a calm conversation she says she hates the first hour of checking scans. Now the tool is "the thing that does the boring hour."

## Common mistakes

Training everyone in one long session. People forget it by Friday.

Ignoring the senior people who have worked there twenty years. Their trust spreads fast.

Measuring success by logins. Measure real work done through the tool.

## On the job

Visit the team in week two without being asked. Small problems are easier to fix before they become stories.
