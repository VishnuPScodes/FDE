## What you're about to do

You'll work out the value of a tool in rupees, set a baseline you can measure later, and write a one page ROI summary for a finance head.

## The idea

ROI means return on investment. In plain words: what did we spend, what did we gain, and how long until we get the money back?

Start with the baseline: how the work looks before. Without a baseline, you cannot prove anything later. Measure it before you start. For example, time per loan file, number of no-shows, hours of data entry.

Then calculate in steps. Hours saved = tasks per month × minutes saved ÷ 60. Money saved = hours saved × cost per hour. Net monthly benefit = money saved − monthly tool cost. Payback in months = one-time setup cost ÷ net monthly benefit.

Example: 400 files a month, 30 minutes saved each, so 200 hours. At ₹350 an hour, that is ₹70,000. If the tool costs ₹25,000 a month, net benefit is ₹45,000. A one-time setup of ₹1,80,000 pays back in 4 months.

Show your working. Mark every guess as an assumption. Finance people trust numbers they can check.

Also name the owner of the number and the review date. "Sunil in finance checks it on the first Monday of each month."

## Common mistakes

Counting saved hours as saved rupees when nobody will be fired or hired less. Say "time freed for other work" if that is the truth.

Forgetting running costs, such as messages, hosting and support time.

Using the best case only. Show a cautious case too.

## On the job

A clear ROI page is often what turns a successful pilot into a signed contract.
