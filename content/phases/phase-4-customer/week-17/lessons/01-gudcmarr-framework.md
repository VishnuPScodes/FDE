## What you're about to do

You'll take a vague customer request and turn it into a plan someone can build. You'll use eight headings, always in the same order. By the end you will do it without thinking about the order.

## The idea

A customer says, "We want AI for our loan files." That is a wish, not a plan. Decomposition means breaking the wish into small, clear parts.

The eight headings are Goal, Users, Data, Constraints, MVP, Architecture, Risks and Rollout. You can remember them as GUDCMARR.

Goal is the business result with one number. "Cut loan file checking from 45 minutes to 15 minutes." Users are the people who touch the system, and what each one needs. Data is what exists, where it lives, and how messy it is. Constraints are budget, privacy law, language, devices and people.

MVP is the smallest thing that moves the number. Architecture is the boxes and arrows, kept short. Risks are what can go wrong and how you reduce each one. Rollout is who tries it first, for how long, and what number decides the next step.

The order matters. Architecture comes after Goal and Data. If you draw boxes before you know the number, you are guessing.

## A small example

A co-operative bank in Thrissur says, "Our staff waste time on loan files."

- Goal: cut checking from 45 to 15 minutes per file by March.
- Users: three loan officers and one branch manager.
- Data: scanned PDFs in a shared folder, some photographed on phones.
- MVP: a checklist that flags missing pages in new files only.

Notice the MVP is tiny. It does not read every field. It only finds missing pages.

## Common mistakes

Writing "improve efficiency" as a goal. There is no number, so nobody can say if you won.

Listing users as "staff". Say which staff, and what each one does today.

Skipping Constraints. In India, personal data rules, local languages and weak mobile data often shape the whole design.

## On the job

When a customer talks, you listen for these eight things. Whatever is missing becomes your next question.
