## What you're about to do

You'll write full decompositions for three different customers, then fix a weak one. The aim is speed, clear numbers, and honest assumptions.

## The idea

You will rarely get every fact. So you write assumptions in plain words. "I assume the clinic has Android phones at the front desk." If the assumption is wrong, the customer will correct you. That is useful.

Keep each heading short. Two or three sentences is usually enough. A reader should be able to skim all eight headings in two minutes.

Practice with a timer. Spend about three minutes on Goal and Users, because everything else depends on them. Then move fast. A complete rough plan beats a perfect Goal with no Rollout.

When you review a weak decomposition, look for four problems. No number in the Goal. An MVP that is really the whole product. Risks that only say "data quality". A Rollout that says "launch" with no pilot.

## A small example

Weak MVP: "Build a full appointment system with reminders, payments and analytics."

Better MVP: "Send a WhatsApp reminder the evening before, for one clinic, for four weeks."

The better version can be built in a week and tested with real patients.

## Common mistakes

Copying the customer's solution. If the clinic owner says "build an app", your MVP may still be a WhatsApp message.

Hiding assumptions. Write them down, even if they feel obvious.

Naming tools too early. Say "a message to patients" before you say a vendor name.

## On the job

In week one with a customer, you will write one of these every few days. The best ones are short, numeric, and easy to argue with.
