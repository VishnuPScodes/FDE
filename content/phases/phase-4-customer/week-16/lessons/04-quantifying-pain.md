## What you're about to do

You'll turn interview notes from a Coimbatore textile exporter into numbers. Then you'll write a problem statement that the operations manager would sign.

## The idea

"It's very painful" won't get a project funded. "It costs ₹2,40,000 a year in staff time and delayed ₹14 lakh of refund last quarter" will.

Pain usually shows up in three ways. Time is hours spent by people. Money is rupees lost, delayed, or written off. Errors are the share of work that goes wrong and has to be redone.

To turn time into money, multiply hours by a fully loaded hourly cost. That is salary plus PF, office space and so on. If you don't know it, ask HR for a rough figure, and say it's an estimate.

Show your maths. A CFO trusts a number they can check.

## A small example

```
2 staff × 25 hours a month = 50 hours a month
50 hours × ₹400 an hour    = ₹20,000 a month
₹20,000 × 12               = ₹2,40,000 a year
```

Add the bigger hit: one wrong shipping bill number held up ₹14 lakh of GST refund for two months. For an exporter, that is a cash flow problem, not just an annoyance.

## The problem statement

A good problem statement names who has the problem, what it is, and how much it costs. It does not name a solution. "They need an AI chatbot" is a solution. "Matching takes 50 hours a month and errors delay refunds" is a problem.

Write down your assumptions. If the ₹400 an hour came from your guess, say so. Then ask the customer to confirm the statement. When Meena says "yes, that's it", you have something to build on.

## Common mistakes

Forgetting to annualise is common. ₹20,000 a month sounds small; ₹2,40,000 a year gets attention.

Mixing up estimates and facts is risky. If a number is a guess and you present it as fact, the CFO will find out, and you lose trust.

Counting the same hours twice happens too. If two clerks share 50 hours, that's 50, not 100.

## On the job

Every number in your problem statement will come back later, in the ROI model and the final demo. Getting it right, and agreed, in week one makes the end of the project much easier.
