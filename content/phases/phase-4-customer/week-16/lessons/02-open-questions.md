## What you're about to do

You'll interview three people in a chat: an operations manager, a CFO and a loan officer. Each one knows facts you need. They will only share them if you ask good questions. You have a limited number of turns, so every question counts.

## The idea

A discovery interview is not a sales pitch. Your job is to learn how work happens today, where it hurts, and what it costs.

Open questions get stories. They start with how, what, or "walk me through". Closed questions get yes or no. "Do you use Tally?" ends the conversation. "Walk me through what happens after an invoice is raised" opens it up.

Leading questions are worse. "Don't you think automation would help?" tells the person what you want to hear. Most people are polite, so they agree. You learn nothing, and you think you learned something.

Ask about real, recent events. "Tell me about last month-end" beats "How is month-end usually?" People remember last Tuesday better than "usually".

Notice when someone names a solution. "We need a chatbot" is a clue, not a requirement. Ask what it would let them do that they can't do today.

## A small example

> You: Can you walk me through what happens after a shipment leaves?
> Meena: Two people copy each shipping bill number into Excel, then match it in Tally.
> You: What happens when something goes wrong in the matching?
> Meena: Last quarter one wrong number held up ₹14 lakh of our GST refund.

Two open questions found a manual step and a ₹14 lakh problem. Compare: "Is matching slow?" "Yes." Done.

## Common mistakes

Pitching too early is the big one. The moment you describe your idea, the person starts reacting to it instead of telling you about their work.

Accepting adjectives is another. "It's very slow" is not a number. Ask how long it takes, how often, and how many people.

Some facts come in layers. A CFO may not talk about cost until he trusts that you understand the volume. Follow the thread instead of jumping around.

In this workspace, a ⚑ flag appears under leading or yes/no questions. Treat it as coaching, not a penalty, but the checks do limit how many you use.

## On the job

The first week at a new customer is mostly interviews. Good notes from these calls become the problem statement, the design doc and later the ROI story. Weak questions now mean building the wrong thing later.
