## What you're about to do

You'll find the root cause of missed appointments at a Pune clinic using five whys. Then you'll write up a morning spent watching a dispatch desk at a logistics company in Kozhikode.

## Five whys

Five whys is simple. Take a problem and ask "why?" Take the answer and ask "why?" again. Keep going until you reach a cause the team can change.

Five is a guide, not a rule. Sometimes you get there in three. Sometimes it takes six. Stop when the answer is something you can fix and test.

Never stop at blame. "Patients are careless" is not a root cause. You can't fix patients. You can fix when the reminder goes out.

## A small example

Sahyadri Family Clinic has a 22% no-show rate.

Why do patients miss visits? They forget. Why? The reminder SMS arrives after 11 pm. Why? The SMS job waits for the billing export. Why does that run so late? It moved to 10 pm when a GST report was added. Root cause: the reminder job depends on the billing schedule. The test: send reminders at 6 pm for two weeks and compare the no-show rate.

## Shadowing

Interviews tell you what people think they do. Shadowing shows you what they actually do. Sit beside someone for an hour. Write down every step, with the time. Say very little.

Watch for workarounds: the WhatsApp group used as a dispatch system, the paper notebook kept "just in case", the printout walked to another desk. Workarounds are where the real process lives. They are also where your tool will win or fail.

Keep observation and opinion apart. "He phoned two drivers to find one truck" is an observation. "They need GPS tracking" is your opinion. Put opinions in their own section at the end.

## Common mistakes

Jumping to a product in the last why is very common. "Why? Because they don't have AI" is not a cause.

Writing notes from memory a day later is another. You will forget the times and the small steps. Write as you watch.

Fixing things while you shadow is tempting. If you show Shabeer an Excel shortcut, you change the workflow you came to see.

## On the job

FDEs often spend their first days on site just watching. A good shadowing note is the most honest input to a design doc, and customers trust you more when they see you took the time to look.
