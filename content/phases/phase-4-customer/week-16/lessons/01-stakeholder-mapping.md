## What you're about to do

You'll map the people around a real project at a co-operative bank in Kerala. You'll name the sponsor, the users, the IT and security people, and the blockers. Then you'll place each of them on a simple grid.

## The idea

Every project has four kinds of people around it.

The sponsor pays for the work and is judged on the result. The users touch your tool every day. IT and security decide if your tool can connect to their systems and data. Blockers can stop or slow you, often late, and often quietly.

The person who asked for the project is not always the sponsor. At a Delhi hospital chain, the billing head may ask for help with insurance claims. But the CFO owns the budget and reports the savings to the board. The CFO is the sponsor.

Once you know who is who, place each person on a power and interest grid. Power means they can change the outcome. Interest means they care about it day to day.

| | Low interest | High interest |
|---|---|---|
| High power | Keep satisfied | Manage closely |
| Low power | Monitor | Keep informed |

## A small example

At Meenachil Service Co-operative Bank, the General Manager asked for a loan document tool. She reports to the board, so she is high power and high interest: manage closely. Thomas in IT must approve any link to core banking. He is high power, so keep him satisfied, and meet him in week one. The loan officers care a lot but can't approve anything: keep them informed, and invite them to test early versions.

## Common mistakes

The first mistake is treating the loudest person as the sponsor. Ask who signs the cheque and who gets blamed if it fails.

The second is forgetting the quiet blocker. The internal auditor never comes to meetings. Then, a week before go-live, you hear "no audit trail, no launch". Find these people early and ask what they need.

The third is writing team names instead of people. "IT" can't answer an email. "Thomas, who runs core banking" can.

## On the job

Forward deployed engineers often fail on people, not code. A stakeholder map on day three saves you from a surprise "no" in month three.
