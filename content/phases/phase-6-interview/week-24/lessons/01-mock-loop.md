## What you're about to do

You'll run through a full interview loop in one week: two customer simulations, a timed decomposition, a behavioural answer, and a debrief. The aim is to feel the real day before it happens.

## The idea

A real loop has several rounds back to back. You get tired, and the later rounds go worse. Practise with the same pressure.

In a customer simulation, the interviewer plays a customer. They are watching how you ask, not what you know. Ask open questions. Stay calm when the customer is angry or vague. Write short, concrete next steps with names and dates.

In a decomposition round, use the eight headings from week 17. Say your assumptions. Keep the MVP small and finish all eight headings.

In a behavioural round, use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Spend most of your time on Action and Result. The result should have a number: hours saved, rupees, percent.

After every mock, write a debrief. What went well? What did you miss? What are three drills for next week? A debrief turns a bad mock into progress.

## A small example

> Interviewer (as customer): Your tool is useless.
> You: Tell me about the last time it let you down.

You did not defend. You asked for a story. That is exactly the skill they are testing.

## Common mistakes

Skipping the debrief. Without it, you repeat the same mistake in the real loop.

Using yes or no questions under stress. Slow down and begin with "what" or "how".

Telling a story with no number. "It went well" does not help the interviewer.

## On the job

Everything here is the daily work of a forward deployed engineer. A good mock loop is just a compressed week on the job.
