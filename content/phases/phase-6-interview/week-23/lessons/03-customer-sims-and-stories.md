## What you're about to do

You will handle an angry CFO and a vague operations head in two simulations. Then you will write your behavioural story bank.

## Customer simulation

Some loops include a live role-play. The interviewer plays an upset or vague stakeholder. They want to see if you stay calm, ask good questions and propose a next step.

With an angry customer, do not defend yourself first. Ask what happened, what it cost and when they need it fixed. Then find the cause together. With a vague customer, ask about the goal, today's process, volumes and constraints before you suggest anything.

## The STAR story bank

Behavioural rounds ask about ambiguity, failure, conflict and impact. Answer with STAR. Situation: the context in one or two sentences. Task: what you owned. Action: what you did, using "I", not "we". Result: what changed, with a number if you can.

Prepare eight stories. Use your capstone and your projects. One story can cover two themes, but you need variety.

## A small example

```text
S: Clinic pilot, week 1. Older patients misread English reminders.
T: I owned the reminder system.
A: I logged a change request, deferred a report, shipped
   Marathi templates in 2 days.
R: Wrong-day visits stopped; no-shows halved by week 4.
```

## Common mistakes

Stories with no result are the most common problem. "And then we finished the project" is not a result.

Saying "we" for every action hides what you did. Interviewers will ask "what did you do?" anyway.

## On the job

Customer calls go wrong in real life too. The habits you build here, calm questions and clear next steps, are the ones that save accounts.
