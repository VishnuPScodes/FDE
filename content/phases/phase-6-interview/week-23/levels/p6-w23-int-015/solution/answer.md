# Story bank

## Ambiguity

Capstone. A Pune clinic asked for "fewer booking problems". I timed reminder calls and counted no-shows to find the real issue. I wrote a one-page scope with one metric. The owner agreed, and the vague ask became a clear target.

## Failure

Capstone. My first English-only reminders confused older patients and two came on the wrong day. I apologised, asked about language and shipped Marathi templates in two days. I now test messages with each user group before launch.

## Pushback on a Customer

Capstone. The clinic owner wanted payments and exercise videos added mid-pilot. I explained it would delay the metric we agreed on. I put both in Out of Scope as next steps, and she agreed.

## Shipped Under Pressure

Project A. A demo for a mock customer was due in two days and the Tally export format changed. I wrote a parser for both formats with tests, cut a dashboard I had planned and shipped on time with a note on what was missing.

## Learned a Domain Fast

Project A. I knew nothing about GST. I read two GSTR-3B guides, asked a CA friend for 30 minutes and built a glossary. Within a week I could explain input tax credit to a reviewer.

## Cross-team Conflict

College project. The front-end team and I disagreed on API field names close to a deadline. I proposed a short shared schema doc and a 15-minute call. We agreed in one meeting and stopped breaking each other's builds.

## Ethical Call

Capstone. It would have been faster to copy the clinic's real patient sheet to my laptop. I asked for a copy with names replaced by IDs and wrote data rules first. It cost a day, but no personal data left the clinic.

## Measurable Impact

Capstone. No-shows fell from 18% to 9% over four weeks. Morning reminder calls dropped from 3 hours to 40 minutes a day. The clinic still runs the system after handover.
