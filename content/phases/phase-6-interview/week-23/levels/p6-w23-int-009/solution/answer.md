# STAR stories

## Ambiguity

Situation: A Pune physiotherapy clinic told me they "wanted fewer problems with bookings". Nobody knew what the real problem was.
Task: I had three weeks to find one problem worth solving and fix it.
Action: I sat at the front desk one Monday, timed 41 reminder calls and counted no-shows from four weeks of the booking sheet. I wrote a one-page scope with one metric and got the owner to agree on WhatsApp.
Result: The vague request became one clear target. No-shows fell from 18% to 9% in four weeks.

## Failure

Situation: In week one of the same pilot, two older patients misread my English reminder and came on the wrong day.
Task: I owned the reminder messages, so this was my mistake.
Action: I called both patients to apologise, then asked the front desk what language patients preferred. I added Marathi templates in two days and logged it as a change request.
Result: Wrong-day visits stopped. I learned to test messages with real users from each group before launch, and I now do that on every project.
